# Red-team audit: `query-notebooklm` skill

Adversarial review, 2026-09-07. All five target files read in full (`SKILL.md`,
`references/corpus-acquisition.md`, `references/notebook-setup.md`,
`references/reading-a-report.md`, `references/round-template.md`), plus `.kiro/traps.md`.
Character counts and cross-file figures were verified with a pure-ASCII PowerShell pass that
extracted the four-backtick fenced blocks and counted them (LF-joined); grep confirmed each quoted
string. No file was edited except this report.

## Verdict

The skill is unusually thorough and self-aware, and its two most-quoted numbers check out exactly
(the acquisition skeleton is **1513** characters as claimed; the query skeleton is **952** as claimed).
I found **no single-step catastrophic defect** and **no dangling/spliced sentence** in any pasteable
block — the precedent failure does not recur. But the pasteable artefacts and the navigation to them
carry **three Major defects that each let a competent, literal follower produce a wrong result**: (1)
the one pasteable acquisition prompt omits the title clause the skill twice says belongs in *every*
acquisition prompt, whose absence the skill itself measured producing unfindable citations; (2)
*neither* pasteable contract contains the confirmation-by-echo clause that `reading-a-report.md`
calls "the highest-value line in the contract," and the query skeleton lacks even the weaker "name
both" version — in a workflow where a self-quoting report is *always* auto-added to the corpus; and
(3) `SKILL.md` steps 4 and 5 give opposite instructions on whether the output contract is pasted into
the round or lives only in custom instructions, while step 4 points to the wrong file for its wording
and the agent cannot set custom instructions itself. Six Minor defects follow, mostly stale or
conflated figures in the "figures this skill owns" table and one inaccurate reference pointer
("nine-check list" for a list of eleven). The reasoning content of `reading-a-report.md` is sound; the
damage is concentrated in what an agent copies and where it is told to look.

## What I tried that did not find a defect

- **Truncation / spliced-sentence hunt in every paste block.** Extracted and measured all four-backtick blocks. The acquisition skeleton (1513 chars), the query skeleton (952 with placeholders, 905 without), the custom-instructions block (703), and the worked round (1248) each begin and end on a complete line. No dangling half-sentence, no two skeletons spliced together, no block over the 3800/5000 limits. The worked round at 1248 is safely pasteable whole.
- **Stated counts against reality.** 1513 and 952 (query skeleton, template form with `<question>` placeholders) match exactly; only the custom-instructions "952" is wrong (703) — reported above.
- **Input-limit consistency.** 3800 (NotebookLM box), 5000 (Deep Research), aim 3600/4800, and the ~1200 under-load floor are consistent across `SKILL.md`, `round-template.md`, `corpus-acquisition.md` and `notebook-setup.md`.
- **The "six things the output contract demands."** `SKILL.md`'s six items map one-to-one onto the six lines of the `round-template.md` query skeleton; that pointer is accurate.
- **Tier vocabulary.** The `[META]`/`[STUDIE]`/`[ANDRAHAND]`/`[BOK]`/`[HEURISTIK]`/`[OKÄND PROVENIENS]`/`[OTÄCKT]` set in `SKILL.md` is internally consistent, each tier is defined, the three meanings of `[OTÄCKT]` are enumerated, and the "add a tier when the corpus needs one" guidance is coherent.
- **Reasoning content of `reading-a-report.md`.** Confirmation-by-echo, number-drift/sign-reversal, the index-shifted table, cross-attribution and the two reasoning defects are each backed by a specific measured instance and are internally consistent. Each of the eleven checks does cite something it caught, so the "caught something" claim holds even though the ordering claim does not.
- **"Trust the number from one answer" (SKILL Verify §1) vs `reading-a-report.md` checks 6-7.** I probed this as a contradiction. It is reconcilable: Verify §1 is scoped to run-to-run reproducibility (one ask vs two for provenance), while checks 6-7 are about report-vs-primary fidelity. Worth watching as a bad-incentive wording risk — a reader could misread "trust the number" as "a number is trustworthy" and skip the direction/table-order check — but I did not classify it as a defect.
- **Tier-to-rule-tier mapping.** The corpus tiers here and the rule tiers referenced in the Verify section belong to a separate skill that is out of scope; I did not pursue whether the two vocabularies are mapped.
