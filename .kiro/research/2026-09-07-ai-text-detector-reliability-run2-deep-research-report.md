(2023) [cite: 2]. (2023) [cite: 3, 4]. (2023) [cite: 1, 5]. (2023) [cite: 2, 6]. (2024) [cite: 9, 10]. (2023) [cite: 18].Single-source claimsNeurodivergent Writing MisclassificationThe claim that automated text detectors systematically misclassify the writing of autistic authors as machine-generated is documented exclusively in the primary corpus study by Summer Chambers and Matthew C. Kelley (2025) [cite: 8]. Their analysis, conducted using the OpenAI GPT-2 detection model on approximately 60,000 Reddit posts, revealed a statistically significant elevation in false positive flags within the likely-autistic subcorpus [cite: 7, 8]. Specifically, posts from autism-focused discussion boards had a 50% greater chance of being flagged (odds-ratio of 1.50) compared to general forum posts [cite: 7].Linguistically, this bias emerges because autistic writers often employ highly structured, precise, and formulaic syntactic patterns, which detectors misinterpret as low-perplexity, machine-like prose [cite: 19, 20]. Incorporating stylistic and semantic properties directly into the generation logit constraint ensures that the synthetic signal remains readable even after severe text editing or machine-assisted paraphrasing [cite: 16].Not documentedThe empirical research literature contains significant gaps where evidence is either thin or entirely absent. First, there is no peer-reviewed primary study examining the robustness of statistical watermarks to cross-lingual translation. Because statistical watermarking relies on embedding green-list token constraints during generation based on localized hash values of preceding tokens [cite: 17, 25], translating the output into another language completely reorganizes the syntactic structures and vocabulary choices. This token-space shift theoretically erases the green-list signal, but no empirical academic studies have quantified this degradation or tested certified robust watermarking under translation attacks.Second, while several evaluations note that post-hoc detectors fail to generalize across languages, the exact numeric drop in accuracy or F1 score when an English-trained detector is applied to non-English corpora (such as German, Spanish, or Chinese) is thin and inconsistently measured across the primary literature, despite general qualitative failure being observed in multilingual benchmarks like MULTITuDE [cite: 9].Third, there is no independent, peer-reviewed replication that supports the 99%+ accuracy and near-zero false positive claims published by commercial detector vendors under realistic, out-of-domain, or adversarial conditions.
