By creating two parallel corpora, the Human-AI Parallel English (HAP-E) and the COCA AI Parallel (CAP), containing human-authored texts and model-generated continuations across six genres, the analysis demonstrated that models systematically fail to match human stylistic range [cite: 1, 8]. Instead, models show a distinct bias toward an informationally dense, noun-heavy academic style [cite: 1]. For example, instruction-tuned models used nominalizations at 1.5 to 2 times the rate of humans and overproduced present participial clauses [cite: 1]. These grammatical patterns remained stable when moving from smaller to larger model sizes within the same family, indicating that parameter scaling does not inherently resolve stylistic divergence [cite: 1, 9]. Crucially, the analysis revealed that instruction tuning, rather than aligning models to write more like humans, actually amplifies these non-human stylistic traits, making instruction-tuned models easier to distinguish from human writing than their untuned base counterparts [cite: 1, 9].Multidimensional Benchmarking of Czech and English Model CorporaThe structural and register shifts of machine-generated text were measured on a broader cross-linguistic scale by Milička, Marklová, and Cvrček (2025) using Biber’s Multidimensional Analysis (MDA) framework [cite: 10, 11]. They constructed two multi-genre corpora of model-generated texts: AI-Brown for English (comparable to the BE21 contemporary British English corpus) and AI-Koditex for Czech (comparable to the original Czech multi-register Koditex corpus) [cite: 10, 12]. By extracting syntactic, morphological, and lexical features, the researchers established an interpretable stylistic benchmark based on normalized shift vector lengths [cite: 10]. This vector represents how far a model's stylistic profile diverges from human reference text in a multidimensional space [cite: 10].The benchmark revealed that instruction-tuned models systematically gravitate toward a highly informational, non-abstract, and explicitly referenced stylistic attractor, while underproducing features of conversational involvement [cite: 13]. The study leveraged established linguistic indicators of "translationese"—the structural footprint left during translation, such as over-explicitation and syntactic simplification—to analyze text generated by Gemini-2.0-Flash-001 and Llama-3.3-70B-Instruct [cite: 3, 15]. Comparing model outputs to native human writing and direct machine-translated corpora in five languages, the analysis proved that model-generated text carries a distinct translationese signature [cite: 3]. This interference is particularly elevated in non-English target languages, including German and Spanish, supporting the hypothesis that multilingual models pass tokens through an internal English representation space during inference [cite: 3].Longitudinal Stylistic Shifts in Academic SettingsThe widespread availability of consumer LLMs has led to measurable, long-term shifts in human writing styles, as documented by Mak and Walasek (2025) in a longitudinal analysis of 4,820 undergraduate psychology reports in the United Kingdom [cite: 5]. Evaluating reports submitted between 2021 and 2025, the researchers tracked lexical markers previously associated with ChatGPT, nominalization rates, syntactic complexity, and readability [cite: 5]. The analysis showed a sharp increase in nominalizations, syntactic complexity, and specific model-associated terms during 2023 and 2024, followed by a slight decline in 2025 [cite: 5]. Despite these prominent surface-level stylistic changes, the study found that assigned grades and tutor feedback remained entirely stable, indicating that adopting machine-like syntactic features does not improve the analytical quality or educational outcome of academic reports [cite: 5].Register Calibration and the Epanorthosis IndexIn an investigation of rhetorical style, Federico Boggia (2025) measured how models handle context-specific rhetorical figures by evaluating "epanorthosis" (intentional self-correction) across three model sizes [cite: 16]. By tracking the density of this figure relative to human baselines, the study established an Epanorthosis Index to assess register calibration [cite: 16]. The findings showed that instruction-tuned models exhibit a severe, bidirectional register mis-calibration [cite: 16]. In formal oratory registers, models drastically overproduced the figure, overshooting the human rate by approximately twofold in English and nearly threefold in Italian [cite: 16]. The factor analysis demonstrated that machine-generated texts are significantly more informationally dense, explicit, and less involved than student-authored assignments [cite: 5]. Utilizing a rotated three-factor solution on 768 balanced text blocks, the study demonstrated that nominal and clausal complexity features successfully differentiate constrained Finnish (including translated and learner varieties) from native Finnish [cite: 6]. The analysis also noted that constrained Finnish varieties display a structural insensitivity to register differences [cite: 6]. Additionally, a translation baseline for North Sámi to Swedish was established in a master's thesis at the University of Gothenburg (2020) [cite: 7].
